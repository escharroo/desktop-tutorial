System readiness · the machine

Priorities, Ownership, Accountability and Communication. These are mechanisms. They either exist or they do not, and building one is a design task with a known cost and a known duration. Mechanisms are cheap relative to culture work, so check them first.

People readiness · the fire

Are your people capable, committed and enabled? Their capacity determines whether the machine runs at all, and it sets the ceiling on how well any mechanism can perform. The three feed each other: knowledge turned into action kindles belief, daily habits produce evidence that deepens understanding, and the loop runs only as strongly as its weakest element.

Build the machine. Light the fire. Neither works without the other.

There is no total score. A constraint does not average out. A system with one broken mechanism and six strong ones performs like the broken one, so a combined number would hide precisely what you came here to find. Read the seven separately, and look at the shape of the two rows before you look at any single figure.

What a self-score cannot see

You have one half of the diagnosis. Send this to three people who have to deliver the strategy — one manager, one specialist, one supervisor — and ask them to score it without seeing your answers.

The gap between your answers is more diagnostic than any of the answers. In my experience, leaders score a level or two above their teams on the same element, and that delta is where execution quietly stalls. It is also the part nobody can do alone, because people rarely score their own leadership honestly to that leadership's face.

What it looks like when both layers work

Nobody is chasing updates. The review is where decisions get made rather than where progress gets reported. And when conditions change, the plan changes within a fortnight, because the people who saw it first had the authority to move.

What did last year's stalled initiatives cost you?

In delays, in rework, in benefits that were signed off and never landed. Most leaders have never put a number on it, and the number is usually uncomfortable. Which means you are not paying for a fix so much as recovering what you have already lost.
